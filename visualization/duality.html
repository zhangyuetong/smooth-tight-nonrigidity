A change of coordinates, not a new surface

Paper · actual content

Differential Legendre duality uses an injective gradient, with

y=∇G(p),f(y)=p⋅y−G(p),∇f(y)=p.y=\nabla G(p),\qquad f(y)=p\cdot y-G(p),\qquad \nabla f(y)=p.y=∇G(p),f(y)=p⋅y−G(p),∇f(y)=p.

It applies to indefinite Hessians; it is not a convex supremum transform.

What this step does

In physical coordinates the original support surface is

Mechanism and mathematical details

At the circular seam,

h(θ)=f(Reθ),b(θ)=fr(Reθ)=Pout⋅eθ>0,h(\theta)=f(Re_\theta),\quad b(\theta)=f_r(Re_\theta)=P_{\rm out}\cdot e_\theta>0,h(θ)=f(Reθ​),b(θ)=fr​(Reθ​)=Pout​⋅eθ​>0,
h′′+Rb=R ∂θPout⋅Jeθ>0.h''+Rb=R\,\partial_\theta P_{\rm out}\cdot Je_\theta>0.h′′+Rb=R∂θ​Pout​⋅Jeθ​>0.

These are precisely the two boundary signs used in the filling. Matching hhh and bbb also matches the angular derivative h′h'h′, hence the entire first jet. The seam data are extracted from the actual outgoing connector, not prescribed independently.

The inverse map exists because the gradient is globally injective on the relevant annulus. Indefinite Hessian alone would give only local inverses and would not authorize this passage.
