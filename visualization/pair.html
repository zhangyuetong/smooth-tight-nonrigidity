What this step does

The two local colored patches are obtained by adding and subtracting the same evaluated supported field. Outside its support they coincide with the base surface, so the exit collars, connectors and radial ends are identical. The global work can therefore be done once and carried unchanged to both branches.

The rigid marker is what prevents an ambient motion from identifying the two different supported branches. Smallness keeps each branch in the embedded tight class after smooth completion.

Mechanism and mathematical details

Expanding the metric gives

⟨d(X+tY),d(X+tY)⟩=⟨dX,dX⟩+t(⟨dX,dY⟩+⟨dY,dX⟩)+t2⟨dY,dY⟩.\langle d(X+tY),d(X+tY)\rangle =\langle dX,dX\rangle+ t\bigl(\langle dX,dY\rangle+\langle dY,dX\rangle\bigr) +t^2\langle dY,dY\rangle.⟨d(X+tY),d(X+tY)⟩=⟨dX,dX⟩+t(⟨dX,dY⟩+⟨dY,dX⟩)+t2⟨dY,dY⟩.

The middle term vanishes identically. The surviving quadratic term is even in ttt, which proves equality for the two endpoints. It does not say that the entire path has the original base metric.

Ver500 first builds the smooth tight torus carrying an infinite-dimensional protected bending kernel, then chooses a sufficiently small nonzero supported field. The finite first pair is the current objective. Later disjoint supports and Cantor families are retained in the manuscript reader but are not used to justify this displayed pair.
