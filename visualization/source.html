43\begin{abstract}
44We construct two noncongruent smooth embedded tight tori with the same
45intrinsic metric that agree on a nonempty open set. Neither torus contains
46an open planar patch. In fact, one metric admits a Cantor family of
47pairwise noncongruent tight realizations agreeing on the same open set.
48The construction starts with a ruled saddle band whose full asymptotic
49return map is the identity. We characterize its compactly supported
50infinitesimal bendings by arbitrary smooth functions of a transverse leaf
51parameter. Opposite signs of a bending give exactly the same metric;
52disjoint supports allow independent sign choices.
53
54The geometric work is to complete this band while leaving its bending
55region fixed. A closure-preserving adjustment of speed cancels the return
56period, and an analytic corrugation supplies the embedding and visibility
57conditions needed for completion. The completion uses a visible connector followed by a single radial
58filling principle in differential Legendre coordinates. This avoids
59straightening both source and gradient, as well as a degenerate cap
60inversion. The resulting support has explicit parabolic end germs,
61which close to a saddle annulus and a tight torus. The same construction yields
62isometric noncongruent saddle annuli with identical boundary collars, an
63annulus with injective Gauss map and closed asymptotic curves of zero normal
64linking, and a complete properly embedded negatively curved cylinder with
65total absolute curvature $4\pi$. These conclusions answer several existing
66questions about tight rigidity and closed asymptotic curves.
67\end{abstract}

70\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
71\subsection{Localized failure of metric reconstruction}
72A surface's intrinsic metric records lengths and angles, but need not
73determine its placement in $\R^3$. The question is especially natural for
74tight surfaces: these minimize total absolute curvature and satisfy the
75two-piece property. Analytic tight surfaces are rigid, and smooth rigidity
76is known under additional hypotheses \cite{BK,Nirenberg}. We show that
77smooth tight tori can nevertheless have distinct realizations of the same
78metric, even when the realizations agree on an open set.
79
80Here two embedded surfaces are \emph{congruent} if an ambient Euclidean
81isometry carries one image onto the other. The following statement therefore
82rules out congruence of images, as well as congruence of parametrized maps.
83Write $g_{\rm Euc}$ for the Euclidean metric.
84
85\begin{theorem}[Localized nonuniqueness for tight tori]\label{thm:main-fiber}
86There are a smooth metric $g$ on $T^2$ and two smooth tight embeddings
87$X_+,X_-:T^2\to\R^3$ such that
88\[
89 X_+^*g_{\rm Euc}=X_-^*g_{\rm Euc}=g.
90\]
91Their images are noncongruent, they agree on a nonempty open subset, and
92neither has an open planar subset.
93
94In fact, the same metric can be chosen to admit a continuous family
95\[
96 \varepsilon\longmapsto X_\varepsilon\in\Emb_g(T^2,\R^3),
97 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb N},
98\]
99of tight embeddings with pairwise noncongruent images. All members agree
100on the same nonempty open set, have no open planar subset, and have total
101absolute curvature $8\pi$. The induced map into
102$\Emb_g(T^2,\R^3)/\Isom(\R^3)$ is a topological embedding.
103\end{theorem}
104
105Here $\Emb_g(M,\R^3)$ is the space of smooth embeddings inducing $g$, with
106the $C^\infty$ topology; $\Isom(\R^3)$ acts by postcomposition. The sign
107space has its product topology and is homeomorphic to the Cantor set.
108The open set in the theorem is fixed by the construction; the statement
109does not prescribe an arbitrary open set in advance.
110
111\subsection{Why localized bendings give exact pairs}
112The algebra behind the result is short. For an immersion $X$ and a vector
113field $y:M\to\R^3$, the metric satisfies the exact identity
114\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-quadratic}
115 g_{X+y}=g_X+S_Xy+\langle dy,dy\rangle,
116\end{equation}
117where $S_Xy$ is the linearized metric change. If $S_Xy=0$, then
118$g_{X+y}=g_{X-y}$. Thus a nonzero infinitesimal bending gives an exact
119sign ambiguity, provided both perturbed maps remain embeddings. Their
120common metric is generally different from $g_X$.
121
122If finitely many such fields have disjoint supports, then
123\[
124 g_{X+\sum_{j=1}^m\varepsilon_j a_jy_j}
125 =g_X+\sum_{j=1}^m a_j^2\langle dy_j,dy_j\rangle,
126 \qquad \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}.
127\]
128This is the finite sign mechanism. The Cantor family follows by choosing
129countably many disjoint fields and rapidly decreasing amplitudes. The
130central issue is therefore geometric: construct a tight torus carrying
131localized bendings in a compact negatively curved region, and eliminate
132ambient symmetries on the region that stays fixed.
133
134\subsection{The band that carries the construction}
135Our bending region lies in a principal-normal ruled band
136\[
137 X(s,u)=\gamma(s)+uE(s).
138\]
139Here $s$ is arclength along a closed curve, and the periodic frame
140$(T,E,n)$ satisfies $T_s=kE$, $E_s=-kT+\tau n$, and $n_s=-\tau E$,
141with $\tau\ne0$. The band has strictly negative Gaussian curvature.
142Put $\rho=\sqrt{|\tau|}$ and $D=k_s-k\tau_s/\tau$. Its nonruling
143asymptotic curves have return map
144\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-return}
145 \mathcal R(u)=\frac{u}{1+\rho(0)I_0u},
146 \qquad I_0=\oint\frac{D}{2\rho}\,ds.
147\end{equation}
148The multiplier $\mathcal R'(0)$ is always one. The stronger condition
149$I_0=0$ makes the full return map the identity, so nearby characteristics
150are closed and admit a single-valued transverse parameter.
151Theorem~\ref{thm:ruled} proves that this condition is exactly what permits
152compactly supported infinitesimal bendings on a thin one-sided band. In
153the identity case, every such bending comes from one arbitrary transverse
154test function.
155
156Producing that band and completing it are separate tasks. For a prescribed
157spherical normal loop, spatial closure is a vector moment condition,
158whereas identity return is one scalar period condition.
159Theorem~\ref{thm:normal-loop-criterion} separates them and cancels the
160period without losing closure. The explicit corrugated seed supplies
161additional embedding and two-sided visibility properties. We then protect
162a compact subannulus, alter the support only outside it, and attach the
163remaining geometry.

165\subsection{Further consequences and earlier questions}
166The same protected band has two useful completions besides the tight torus.
167\begin{theorem}[Closed asymptotic annulus]\label{thm:main-annulus}
168There is a smooth embedded compact annulus $N\subset\R^3$ with $K<0$
169in its interior and circular boundary components lying in tangent planes.
170For a suitable normal, its Gauss map is a diffeomorphism
171\[
172 N^\circ\longrightarrow\Sph^2\setminus\{\pm e_3\}.
173\]
174An open subannulus is foliated by embedded closed asymptotic curves.
175Each has linking number zero with a sufficiently small normal push-off.
176\end{theorem}
177
178\begin{theorem}[Complete embedded saddle cylinder]\label{thm:complete-cylinder}
179There is a smooth proper embedding $X_\infty:S^1\times\R\to\R^3$
180whose image $\Sigma$ has complete induced metric and $K<0$ everywhere.
181It contains an open annulus of embedded closed asymptotic curves with
182identity return and zero normal linking. A choice of normal gives
183\[
184 n:\Sigma\longrightarrow\Sph^2\setminus\{\pm e_3\}
185 \quad\text{a diffeomorphism},\qquad
186 \int_\Sigma|K|\,dA=4\pi.
187\]
188For some nonempty interval $I$, there is an injective linear family of
189infinitesimal bendings parametrized by $C_c^\infty(I)$, all supported in
190one fixed compact subset of $\Sigma$.
191\end{theorem}
192
193These results address the following questions through a single mechanism.
194\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
195\item Da Cruz--Garcia ask for a cylindrical region foliated by closed
196asymptotic lines \cite{DaCruzGarcia}. The identity-return band supplies one.
197\item Nirenberg's annulus question, recorded in \cite[\S1.2]{GhomiProblems},
198asks whether negative curvature and convex planar boundaries allow a closed
199asymptotic curve. Theorem~\ref{thm:main-annulus} gives a whole band.
200\item Ghomi--Raffaelli ask for a closed asymptotic curve with zero normal
201linking on an embedded negative-curvature surface with injective Gauss map
202\cite[Problem~1.1]{GR}. The same annulus supplies both conditions. Its
203normal along a leaf is an injective binormal field, also giving a negative
204answer to \cite[Problem~1.4]{GhomiProblems}.
205\item Banchoff--K\"uhnel and Ghomi ask about smooth tight rigidity
206\cite[\S1.6, Question~11]{BK} and \cite[Problem~1.2]{GhomiProblems}.
207Theorem~\ref{thm:main-fiber} gives noncongruent isometric embedded tori.
208Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-boundary-annuli} similarly answers the
209fixed-boundary annulus question in \cite[Problem~1.3]{GhomiProblems}.
210\item The tight torus contains a continuum of closed asymptotic curves in
211one negatively curved component. This tests the proposed restriction
212discussed by Yau \cite{YauReview}. The Han--Khuri return integral vanishes
213on every leaf (Corollary~\ref{cor:HK-degenerate}), answering their question
214about whether tightness forces nonvanishing \cite{HK}.
215\item Ni--Zhang--Zhang construct a complete immersed example and ask for
216an embedded one \cite[after Theorem~3.4]{NiZhangZhang}.
217Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-cylinder} provides a proper embedded cylinder.
218\end{enumerate}
219
220The Han--Khuri rigidity theorems also impose curvature-transition
221hypotheses; our example does not contradict those theorems. Nor does a
222Cantor family settle the Euler--Maxwell question about a continuous smooth
223flex of a closed surface \cite[Problem~1.1]{GhomiProblems}.

225\subsection{Relation to earlier constructions}
226Kovaleva already used principal-normal rulings to obtain a negatively
227curved strip containing one closed asymptotic curve \cite{Kovaleva};
228periodic asymptotic returns were studied by Garcia--Sotomayor
229\cite{GarciaSotomayor}. The ruling and the existence of a closed leaf are
230therefore established ideas. Here the full return is the identity, the
231supported bending space is explicitly classified, and the band is retained
232through global completions.
233
234Noncongruent negatively curved patches agreeing on an open set were
235constructed by Sabitov \cite{Sabitov2018}. Sa Earp--Toubiana obtained
236uncountable isometric families of closed surfaces, including Cantor
237families, using infinitely flat planar seams \cite{SaEarpToubiana};
238Sabitov later constructed metrics admitting a discontinuum of
239noncongruent immersions \cite{Sabitov2021}. Our contribution is localized
240exact reconstruction failure within smooth embedded tight tori with no
241open planar patch, together with the geometric band that makes this
242possible. Ghomi--Raffaelli analyzed topology and normal linking of closed
243asymptotic curves, including restrictions from Gauss-map injectivity
244\cite{GR}; our annulus has both zero normal linking and an injective
245Gauss map.

279\section{Conventions and geometric tools}\label{sec:prelim}
280\subsection{Metrics, bendings, and tightness}
281All maps are smooth unless stated otherwise. A compact annulus includes
282its boundary; $A^\circ$ denotes its interior. For an open interval or
283surface, $C_c^\infty$ denotes smooth functions with compact support.
284Writing $V\Subset U$ means that $V$ is compact and contained in $U$.
285Norms on compact sources use fixed smooth atlases or a fixed background
286metric. For an oriented immersion $X$ with unit normal $N$, we use
287\[
288 g_X(v,w)=\langle dX(v),dX(w)\rangle,\qquad
289 \II_X(v,w)=\langle D_v(dX(w)),N\rangle.
290\]
291Here $D$ is the Euclidean connection. The strain is the symmetric tensor
292\[
293 S_Xy(v,w)=\langle dX(v),dy(w)\rangle+
294 \langle dX(w),dy(v)\rangle.
295\]
296An \emph{infinitesimal bending} is a field $y:M\to\R^3$ with $S_Xy=0$;
297this definition allows both tangential and normal components. We write
298$\Gmap(X)=g_X$ for the metric reconstruction map. The tensor
299$\langle dy,dy\rangle$ means $(v,w)\mapsto\langle dy(v),dy(w)\rangle$.
300
301An asymptotic direction is a nonzero vector $v$ with $\II_X(v,v)=0$.
302When $K<0$, these directions form two transverse smooth line fields.
303A \emph{return map} follows one such field once around a closed leaf,
304starting and ending on a short transverse arc. Identity return means
305identity of the whole germ, rather than only its first derivative.
306
307A closed connected embedded surface is \emph{tight} if its intersection
308with each open half-space is connected whenever nonempty. Its total
309absolute curvature satisfies \cite[\S1.1]{BK}
310\begin{equation}\label{eq:tight-bound}
311 \int_M|K|\,dA\ge2\pi(4-\chi(M)),
312 \qquad\text{with equality exactly for tight embeddings}.
313\end{equation}
314For a torus the equality value is $8\pi$.
315
316We repeatedly use openness of compact embeddings in the $C^1$ topology.
317Local projections to tangent planes exclude collisions between nearby
318source points, uniformly on a finite cover; compactness separates the
319remaining pairs. The same argument shows that an immersion injective on
320a compact embedded curve is injective on a sufficiently thin neighborhood
321of that curve.
322
323For an embedded closed curve $\Gamma$ and a nonzero field $\nu$ transverse
324to its tangent, define
325\[
326 \Lk(\Gamma,\nu)=\Lk(\Gamma,\Gamma+\epsilon\nu)
327\]
328for small $\epsilon>0$. We compute linking by intersection with an
329oriented spanning surface \cite{Rolfsen}.

331\subsection{Support coordinates}\label{sec:support}
332The completion is easiest to control by using the normal as the source
333coordinate. Let $q\in\Omega\subset\Sph^2$, with round metric $g_0$, and
334let $H:\Omega\to\R$. Define
335\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
336 a_H=\nabla H+Hq,\qquad B_H=\nabla^2H+H\Id,
337 \qquad R_H=\det B_H.
338\end{equation}
339The gradient and Hessian in this formula are spherical. We use $g_0$ to
340identify symmetric tensors with self-adjoint endomorphisms.
341
342\begin{proposition}[Support reconstruction]\label{prop:support}
343The $\R^3$-valued differential of $a_H$ is $B_H$. Where $B_H$ is
344invertible, $a_H$ is an immersion with normal $q$, metric $B_H^2$,
345second fundamental form $-B_H$, and curvature $R_H^{-1}$. Every immersion
346with an injective regular Gauss map has this representation on its Gauss
347image.
348\end{proposition}
349\begin{proof}
350Differentiating $\nabla H$ in $\R^3$ produces the normal term
351$-dH\,q$, which cancels the corresponding term from $d(Hq)$.
352The remaining differential is $\nabla^2H+H\Id$. It is tangent to
353$q^\perp$, so $q$ is normal. Differentiating $da_H(w)\cdot q=0$ gives
354$\II(v,w)=-\langle B_Hw,v\rangle$. Thus the shape operator is
355$-B_H^{-1}$ and its determinant is $R_H^{-1}$.
356Conversely, in Gauss coordinates set $H=X\cdot q$. Then
357$dH(v)=X\cdot v$, because $dX(v)\cdot q=0$, and the tangential and normal
358parts of $X$ are $\nabla H$ and $Hq$.
359\end{proof}
360
361A self-adjoint field is \emph{Codazzi} when
362$(\nabla_vB)w=(\nabla_wB)v$ for all tangent vectors $v,w$.
363\begin{proposition}[Codazzi realization and periods]\label{prop:codazzi}
364For a smooth self-adjoint field $B$ on $\Omega$, the vector-valued form
365$\beta_B(v)=Bv$ is closed exactly when $B$ is Codazzi. On a simply
366connected domain this is equivalent to $B=B_H$, with $H$ unique modulo
367ambient linear functions. On a general domain one must also require
368\[
369 \oint_\gamma\beta_B=0\quad\text{for every closed curve }\gamma.
370\]
371\end{proposition}
372\begin{proof}
373The normal part of $d\beta_B(v,w)$ is
374$-\langle v,Bw\rangle+\langle w,Bv\rangle=0$ by self-adjointness.
375Its tangential part is $(\nabla_vB)w-(\nabla_wB)v$, the Codazzi defect.
376If the form has a primitive $a$, put $H=a\cdot q$. Since $da$ is tangent,
377$dH(v)=a\cdot v$, hence $a=\nabla H+Hq$ and $B=B_H$.
378The primitive is unique up to a constant vector, which changes $H$ by a
379linear function. Vanishing periods are precisely the obstruction to a
380global primitive.
381\end{proof}
382
383On the northern hemisphere introduce \emph{gnomonic coordinates}
384\[
385 q(z)=\frac{(z,1)}{w(z)},\qquad w(z)=\sqrt{1+|z|^2},
386 \qquad G(z)=w(z)H(q(z)).
387\]
388In these planar source coordinates the immersion is
389\begin{equation}\label{eq:planar}
390 X_G(z)=\bigl(\nabla G(z),G(z)-z\cdot\nabla G(z)\bigr),
391 \qquad K_{X_G}=\frac1{w^4\det D^2G}.
392\end{equation}
393Indeed, writing $A=D^2G$ gives
394\[
395 dX_G(\xi)=(A\xi,-z\cdot A\xi),\qquad
396 g_{X_G}=A^T(I+zz^T)A,\qquad \II=-A/w.
397\]
398Thus $R_H(q(z))=w^4\det D^2G$. A \emph{saddle support} means
399$R_H<0$, equivalently $\det D^2G<0$. Planar gradients below are
400Euclidean, in contrast with the spherical gradient in \eqref{eq:support}.
401
402If $p$ is a planar source curve, let $\gamma=\nabla G\circ p$ be its
403gradient trace and $g=G\circ p$ its value trace. Then
404\begin{equation}\label{eq:action}
405 g'=\gamma\cdot p',\qquad \oint\gamma\cdot dp=0,
406\end{equation}
407and, for $q=q\circ p$,
408\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-sign}
409 B_H(q',q')=\frac{p'\cdot\gamma'}{w(p)}.
410\end{equation}
411We call a trace \emph{positive} when $p'\cdot\gamma'>0$.
412Such a curve is noncharacteristic. A collar is an open tubular
413neighborhood; a germ is a function on a collar, considered up to restriction
414to a smaller collar. Retaining a germ means agreement on an open overlap,
415so all derivatives agree at the eventual join.

417\subsection{Global injectivity, duality, and affine transport}
418Local negative determinant will give local inversion. The following
419degree argument is what upgrades it to global injectivity.
420\begin{lemma}[Annular degree criterion]\label{lem:degree}
421Let $F$ be a smooth map from a compact annulus to $\R^2$. Suppose the
422interior Jacobian has a constant nonzero sign, the boundary maps are
423homeomorphisms onto disjoint nested Jordan curves, and their induced
424winding numbers are opposite. Then $F$ is a diffeomorphism of interiors
425onto the intervening planar annulus and a homeomorphism of closures.
426The boundary Jacobian may vanish.
427\end{lemma}
428\begin{proof}
429For a target off the boundary images, all preimages are interior and the
430degree is defined by compactness. The boundary winding sum has absolute
431value one between the curves and is zero in the complementary regions.
432All local degrees have the same sign, so there is exactly one preimage
433between the curves and none outside. No interior point maps to a boundary
434image: local openness there would produce a target on the forbidden side.
435Together with the boundary hypotheses this gives a continuous bijection
436of compact closures, hence a homeomorphism. Local inversion gives the
437smooth interior inverse.
438\end{proof}
439
440If $\nabla G$ is a diffeomorphism, its \emph{differential Legendre transform}
441is
442\[
443 G^*(y)=z\cdot y-G(z),\qquad y=\nabla G(z).
444\]
445It satisfies $\nabla G^*=z$ and $D^2G^*=(D^2G)^{-1}$, and therefore
446preserves the saddle condition. This is differential duality; it does not
447require convexity.
448
449We fix the planar maps and unit vectors
450\[
451 J(x_1,x_2)=(-x_2,x_1),\qquad S(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,-x_2),
452 \qquad A_*=JS,\qquad e_\theta=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta).
453\]
454Thus $e_\theta'=Je_\theta$ and $SJ=-JS$.
455For an invertible affine map $x\mapsto Ax+b$, the correct transport of
456a bending is
457\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-transport}
458 \widetilde X=AX+b,\qquad \widetilde y=A^{-T}y.
459\end{equation}
460Derivative pairings are unchanged, so $S_{\widetilde X}\widetilde y=S_Xy$.
461The second fundamental form changes by a nonzero scalar, preserving
462curvature signs and asymptotic directions. Affine maps also preserve
463tightness, since inverse images of half-spaces are half-spaces.
464See \cite{IzmProj} for the broader projective rigidity correspondence.

466\section{Identity return and localized bendings}\label{sec:theory}
467\label{sec:ruled}
468This section isolates the local mechanism. Holonomy here means return of
469an asymptotic line field, not Levi--Civita parallel transport. Identity
470return is compatible with strictly negative curvature. It supplies a
471transverse leaf parameter, and the ruled equations turn that parameter
472into the full supported bending space.
473
474\subsection{A transverse coordinate for closed leaves}
475\begin{proposition}[Product and first-integral criterion]\label{prop:product-holonomy}
476For a smooth nonsingular line foliation near an embedded closed leaf in
477an annulus, the following conditions are equivalent after shrinking the
478neighborhood: the return germ is the identity; the foliation is a product
479$S^1\times J$ by circles; there is a smooth submersion whose connected
480fibers are the leaves. Equivalently, a nonvanishing defining one-form has
481a nonvanishing integrating factor that makes it exact.
482\end{proposition}
483\begin{proof}
484Start with a coordinate on a short transversal and extend it by transport
485along leaves. It is single-valued after one circuit exactly when return
486is the identity. It then defines the submersion and the local product.
487A product has identity return. The differential of the submersion is a
488nonzero multiple of any defining form. Conversely, an exact nonvanishing
489defining form is the differential of a transverse coordinate; near the
490original compact leaf, its level sets are circles.
491\end{proof}
492The proposition concerns only the foliation. A general saddle surface
493need not have a supported bending for every transverse function. The
494ruled model below provides that additional fact.
495
496\subsection{The principal-normal model and its return}
497Let $\gamma(s)$ be a unit-speed closed curve of period $L$. Assume its
498periodic positively oriented frame $(T,E,n)$ satisfies
499\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame}
500 T_s=kE,\qquad E_s=-kT+\tau n,\qquad n_s=-\tau E,
501 \qquad \tau\ne0.
502\end{equation}
503The curvature $k$ is signed and may vanish. Set
504\[
505 X(s,u)=\gamma(s)+uE(s),\quad \rho=\sqrt{|\tau|},\quad
506 \lambda=\tau_s/\tau,\quad D=k_s-k\lambda.
507\]
508For the normal determined by $X_s\times X_u$, differentiation gives
509\begin{align}\label{eq:ruled-II}
510 g_X&=\mathcal E\,ds^2+du^2,
511 &\mathcal E&=(1-ku)^2+\tau^2u^2,\\
512 \II_{ss}&=\frac{u\tau(\lambda+uD)}{\sqrt{\mathcal E}},
513 &\II_{su}&=\frac\tau{\sqrt{\mathcal E}},
514 &\II_{uu}&=0.\nonumber
515\end{align}
516In particular $K=-\tau^2/\mathcal E^2<0$. One asymptotic family consists
517of the rulings. The other satisfies
518\[
519 u_s=-\frac u2(\lambda+uD),\qquad
520 \frac d{ds}\frac1{\rho u}=\frac D{2\rho}.
521\]
522The reciprocal coordinate turns the return calculation into a single
523period. Put
524\[
525 I_0=\int_0^L\frac D{2\rho}\,ds.
526\]
527Periodicity of $\rho$ gives
528\begin{equation}\label{eq:return}
529 \mathcal R(u)=\frac{u}{1+\rho(0)I_0u}.
530\end{equation}
531Thus $\mathcal R'(0)=1$ in every case, but the full return is the identity
532only if $I_0=0$.
533
534There is also a transverse coordinate regular at the central leaf.
535Choose periodic $\omega$ with $\omega_s=D/(2\rho)-I_0/L$. Then
536\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-holonomy-coordinate}
537 z=\frac{\rho u}{1-\rho u\omega(s)}
538\end{equation}
539has $z_u(s,0)=\rho(s)>0$ and changes the characteristic field to
540$\partial_s-(I_0/L)z^2\partial_z$. When $I_0=0$, $z$ is a smooth first
541integral across $u=0$; on a one-sided band, $v=z^{-1}$ is more convenient.
542
543\begin{remark}[What the period measures]\label{rem:period-holonomy}
544The form $(D/(2\rho))\,ds$ has one cohomological obstruction to a
545periodic primitive: its integral $I_0$. In this principal-normal class,
546$\mathcal R''(0)=-2\rho(0)I_0$, so the quadratic jet detects the whole
547return. Along the central curve,
548$\II=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\tau\\\tau&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
549in the orthonormal basis $(T,E)$. The transverse normal curvature is zero,
550which explains the automatic multiplier one. This does not say that the
551whole band is minimal.
552\end{remark}

554\subsection{Classification of the supported kernel}
555\begin{theorem}[Supported kernel on a ruled band]\label{thm:ruled}
556On a sufficiently thin one-sided band $0<u<b$, a nonzero compactly
557supported infinitesimal bending exists exactly when $I_0=0$. In that
558case choose periodic $\omega$ with $\omega_s=D/(2\rho)$ and define
559\[
560 v(s,u)=\frac1{\rho(s)u}-\omega(s),\qquad
561 c_b=\max_s\left(\frac1{\rho(s)b}-\omega(s)\right).
562\]
563Every supported bending has a unique expression
564\begin{equation}\label{eq:yF}
565 y_F=\frac{\tau u}{2\rho}F'(v)T+
566 \left[uF(v)-\frac{1-ku}{2\rho}F'(v)\right]n,
567 \qquad F\in C_c^\infty((c_b,\infty)).
568\end{equation}
569\end{theorem}
570\begin{proof}
571\emph{Step 1: reduce the strain to transport.}
572Write $y=\alpha T+\chi E+\beta n$. The $uu$ equation is $\chi_u=0$;
573compact support therefore gives $\chi=0$. The remaining equations are
574\[
575 (1-ku)\alpha_s+u\tau\beta_s=0,\qquad
576 (1-ku)\alpha_u+u\tau\beta_u+k\alpha-\tau\beta=0.
577\]
578For $p=(1-ku)\alpha+u\tau\beta$, these are equivalent to
579\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport-p}
580 \alpha=p-\frac u2p_u,\qquad
581 \beta=\frac{kp+(1-ku)p_u/2}{\tau},\qquad
582 p_s-\frac u2(\lambda+uD)p_u=-uDp.
583\end{equation}
584Consequently $f=p/(\tau u^2)$ is constant along the nonruling
585characteristics.
586
587\emph{Step 2: support forces identity return.}
588Extend the field by zero to $u>0$. A characteristic on which $f\ne0$
589cannot leave the compact support: transport preserves its nonzero value.
590It exists for all $s$ and stays in a compact interval bounded away from
591$u=0$. But $(\rho u)^{-1}$ gains $I_0$ on every circuit, which is
592incompatible with this boundedness unless $I_0=0$.
593
594\emph{Step 3: reconstruct all supported fields.}
595When $I_0=0$, the complete leaves lying in the band are
596\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-leaves}
597 u_c(s)=\frac1{\rho(s)(c+\omega(s))},\qquad c>c_b.
598\end{equation}
599Taking $p=\tau u^2F(v)$ in \eqref{eq:transport-p} gives \eqref{eq:yF}.
600A compact set of levels gives a compact subannulus, so every displayed
601field is supported. Conversely, if a field is supported in
602$u_-\le u\le u_+$ with $0<u_-<u_+<b$, every nonzero level satisfies
603\[
604 \max_s\left(\frac1{\rho(s)u_+}-\omega(s)\right)
605 \le c\le
606 \min_s\left(\frac1{\rho(s)u_-}-\omega(s)\right).
607\]
608The left endpoint is greater than $c_b$ and the right endpoint is finite.
609Evaluation of $f$ at $s=0$ therefore gives a smooth compactly supported
610function $F$ on $(c_b,\infty)$. Transport proves reconstruction, and every
611such level occurs on a complete leaf, proving uniqueness.
612\end{proof}
613We call a subband \emph{saturated} when it is a union of complete leaves.
614Disjoint transverse supports in \eqref{eq:yF} give disjoint surface
615supports. This feature, rather than just the dimension of the kernel,
616is what will permit exact independent sign choices.

618\subsection{The intrinsic multiplier and the Han--Khuri integral}
619For later applications we record what the linear return measures on an
620arbitrary saddle surface.
621\begin{lemma}[Intrinsic return multiplier]\label{lem:holonomy-integral-new}
622Let $\Gamma$ be an embedded closed asymptotic curve on an oriented
623surface with $K<0$ near $\Gamma$. Parametrize it by oriented arclength,
624write $T$ for its tangent and $Z$ for a unit perpendicular tangent, and set
625\[
626 k_g=\langle\nabla_TT,Z\rangle,\qquad k_n=\II(Z,Z),\qquad
627 \sigma=\operatorname{sign}\II(T,Z).
628\]
629If $\mu$ is the derivative of the asymptotic return at $\Gamma$, then
630\begin{equation}\label{eq:holonomy-integral-new}
631 \log\mu=\frac\sigma2\oint_\Gamma k_gk_n|K|^{-1/2}\,ds.
632\end{equation}
633\end{lemma}
634\begin{proof}
635Choose Fermi coordinates $(s,t)$ with metric $h^2ds^2+dt^2$ and
636$h(s,0)=1$, and write $\II=L\,ds^2+2M\,ds\,dt+N\,dt^2$.
637On $t=0$ we have $L=0$, $N=k_n$, and $M=\sigma\sqrt{-K}$.
638Codazzi gives $L_t=M_s-k_gN$. If the asymptotic slope is $t_s=f(s,t)$,
639then $f(s,0)=0$ and $L+2Mf+Nf^2=0$ implies $f_t=-L_t/(2M)$ on the
640leaf. The variational equation yields
641\[
642 \log\mu=-\frac12\oint\frac{L_t}{M}\,ds
643 =-\frac12\oint\frac{M_s}{M}\,ds+
644 \frac12\oint\frac{k_gN}{M}\,ds.
645\]
646The first integral is zero by periodicity. Substitution of $M$ and $N$
647in the second gives the assertion.
648\end{proof}
649The integral in \cite{HK} detects the linear multiplier. Equation
650\eqref{eq:return} explains why multiplier one alone does not imply full
651identity return.

653\section{Closing a normal loop and cancelling its return period}
654\label{sec:structural-balance}
655We now construct the frame used in Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The input is
656a loop of normals, not a spatial curve. A positive speed determines the
657spatial primitive. Closure and return then become two explicit conditions
658on that speed, which can be solved separately.
659
660\subsection{The two conditions on speed}
661Let $\zeta:\R/\mathcal L\mathbb Z\to\Sph^2$ be a regular spherical loop
662parametrized by spherical arclength $r$. Define
663\begin{equation}\label{eq:structural-normal-frame}
664 P=-\zeta\times\zeta_r,\qquad
665 \zeta_{rr}=-\zeta-\kappa P,\qquad P_r=\kappa\zeta_r.
666\end{equation}
667These formulas fix the sign of its geodesic curvature $\kappa$.
668A positive function $a$ determines $\Gamma_r=aP$. If $ds=a\,dr$,
669then the frame $(P,\zeta_r,\zeta)$ has $k=\kappa/a$ and $\tau=-1/a$.
670Thus
671\begin{equation}\label{eq:structural-moments}
672 \mathbf M(a)=\oint aP\,dr=0,\qquad
673 \mathscr B(a)=\oint\frac{\kappa_r}{\sqrt a}\,dr=0,
674 \qquad I_0=\frac12\mathscr B(a).
675\end{equation}
676The first equation closes the spatial curve; the second cancels the full
677return period. Using $-aP$ gives the other torsion sign with the same
678conditions.
679
680\subsection{A local speed correction that preserves moments}
681The next lemma makes an arbitrarily small positional change while
682prescribing a possibly large nonlinear period. Its smallness is in $L^1$
683for the speed, rather than in curvature or torsion norms.
684\begin{lemma}[Finite-moment period prescription]\label{lem:finite-moment-balance}
685Let $f:S^1\to\R^m$ and $g:S^1\to\R$ be smooth, with $g$ taking both
686signs. For every smooth $b>0$, $B_*\in\R$, and $\eta>0$, there is a
687smooth $a>0$ such that
688\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-moment-balance}
689 \|a-b\|_{L^1}<\eta,\qquad
690 \oint af=\oint bf,\qquad \oint g/\sqrt a=B_*.
691\end{equation}
692If $d=\dim\operatorname{span}f(S^1)$, the correction uses at most $d+1$
693arbitrarily short intervals.
694\end{lemma}
695\begin{proof}
696\emph{Choose a slowdown and compensating controls.}
697If the period already equals $B_*$, take $a=b$. Otherwise choose a short
698interval $K$ where $g$ has the sign of $B_*-\oint g/\sqrt b$.
699Choose $d$ disjoint control intervals $O_j$ away from $K$, with smooth
700bumps $\psi_j$ whose moments $\oint\psi_jf$ form a basis of
701$\operatorname{span}f(S^1)$. One can first choose independent sample
702values of $f$, then a center for $K$ in the required sign region away
703from those finitely many points, and finally shrink all intervals.
704Let $0\le\chi\le1$ be supported in $K$ and equal one on a smaller arc.
705Solve
706\[
707 \sum_{j=1}^d c_j\oint\psi_jf=\oint\chi bf,
708\]
709and set, for $0\le t<1$,
710\[
711 a_t=b-t\chi b+t\sum_{j=1}^d c_j\psi_j.
712\]
713This path preserves every linear moment. The argument also covers $d=0$,
714when there are no controls.
715
716\emph{Keep the correction small and prescribe the period.}
717Fixing the controls and shortening $K$ gives $c_j=O(|K|)$. Thus $a_t$
718is positive on the controls and $\|a_t-b\|_{L^1}<\eta$, uniformly in
719$t$. On $K$, positivity follows from $t<1$. On the plateau,
720$a_t=(1-t)b$, so the contribution of $g/\sqrt{a_t}$ diverges with the
721chosen sign as $t\uparrow1$. All contributions on $K$ have that sign;
722those on the complement stay bounded. Continuity therefore attains $B_*$
723at some $t_*<1$. Take $a=a_{t_*}$.
724\end{proof}

726\subsection{The structural existence criterion}
727\begin{theorem}[Normal-loop existence criterion]\label{thm:normal-loop-criterion}
728For the loop in \eqref{eq:structural-normal-frame}, the following are
729equivalent:
730\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
731\item $0$ belongs to the ordinary three-dimensional interior of
732$\conv P(S^1)$;
733\item a smooth positive speed $b$ satisfies $\mathbf M(b)=0$;
734\item a smooth positive speed $a$ satisfies both
735$\mathbf M(a)=0$ and $\mathscr B(a)=0$.
736\end{enumerate}
737This characterizes closed immersed principal-normal identity-return bands
738with the prescribed normal loop. For every closing speed $b$, the balanced
739speed $a$ can be chosen arbitrarily close to $b$ in $L^1$.
740\end{theorem}
741\begin{proof}
742\emph{From the convex hull to a closing speed.}
743Assume (i) and consider
744\[
745 \Phi(\lambda)=\oint e^{\lambda\cdot P(r)}\,dr,
746 \qquad \lambda\in\R^3.
747\]
748Its Hessian is positive definite because $P$ spans $\R^3$. The interior
749condition gives a uniform positive lower bound for
750$\max_r v\cdot P(r)$ as $v$ ranges over unit vectors. Uniform continuity
751then gives arcs of uniformly positive length on which this scalar product
752is bounded below. Hence $\Phi$ is coercive. At its unique minimizer
753$\lambda_*$, the positive speed $b=e^{\lambda_*\cdot P}$ has
754$\mathbf M(b)=\nabla\Phi(\lambda_*)=0$.
755
756\emph{Why positive closure forces the interior condition.}
757Assume (ii). If (i) fails, separation gives a nonzero $v$ with
758$v\cdot P\ge0$. Since $b>0$ and the moment is zero, $v\cdot P=0$
759everywhere. Differentiation gives $\kappa v\cdot\zeta_r=0$.
760On any interval where $\kappa\ne0$, $v$ is perpendicular to both
761$P$ and $\zeta_r$, so $\zeta$ is parallel to $v$. This contradicts
762$|\zeta_r|=1$. Thus $\kappa=0$ everywhere, making $P$ constant;
763a positive speed along a constant unit vector cannot close. This proves (i).
764
765\emph{Cancel the period without changing closure.}
766A closing speed also forces $\kappa$ to be nonconstant. Indeed, if
767$\kappa=c$, then $C=P-c\zeta$ is constant and $P\cdot C=1$, giving
768$\mathbf M(b)\cdot C=\oint b>0$. Therefore $\kappa_r$ takes both signs.
769Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-balance} with $f=P$, $g=\kappa_r$,
770and $B_*=0$. This gives (iii) with arbitrary $L^1$ control; (iii) implies
771(ii) immediately. The frame formulas and \eqref{eq:structural-moments}
772then produce the asserted immersed band, whose curvature is negative.
773\end{proof}

775\begin{corollary}[Relative upgrade of an embedded seed]\label{cor:relative-holonomy-upgrade}
776If a closing speed $b$ has an embedded primitive $\Gamma_b$, the balanced
777primitive can be chosen embedded and arbitrarily uniformly close to it.
778The two are connected through embedded closed curves with the same oriented
779tangent and normal at each parameter value. Any specified regular embedded
780linear projection remains embedded. If $\zeta$ is embedded, the Gauss map
781of a sufficiently thin resulting band is injective.
782\end{corollary}
783\begin{proof}
784Use the same initial point for all primitives. Their uniform distance is
785bounded by the $L^1$ change of speed. On a short parameter arc, some fixed
786linear functional is strictly increasing along every positive-speed
787primitive of $P$; this excludes nearby collisions. Compact separation for
788$\Gamma_b$ excludes all other collisions under a small uniform change.
789The argument holds along the whole correcting path and for a specified
790regular embedded projection. Tangent and normal directions are fixed.
791Finally the ruled Gauss map is locally invertible and injective on the
792compact central curve; it is injective on a thin neighborhood.
793\end{proof}
794An embedded closed asymptotic curve on an oriented $K<0$ surface has
795nonzero geodesic torsion \cite[\S2.2]{GR}. Its normal, parametrized by
796spherical arclength, gives the frame above with one torsion sign. The
797corollary upgrades it to an identity-return ruled band without changing
798its knot type or normal self-linking. It does not deform the original
799surface isometrically. The global completion still needs the two visible
800traces supplied in the next section.

802\begin{remark}[The signed action of speed and curvature]\label{rem:holonomy-action}
803With $\rho=a^{-1/2}$, the return period is $2I_0=\oint\rho\,d\kappa$,
804the signed-area action of $r\mapsto(\kappa(r),\rho(r))$, including its
805algebraic multiplicities. A positive speed law $a=A(\kappa)$ cancels
806this action automatically, but need not solve the independent closing
807equation $\oint A(\kappa)P\,dr=0$. The correction lemma can prescribe
808any return period while preserving closure; adding $1$ to the linear
809moments also preserves total length. The positional control remains
810$C^0$, obtained from $L^1$ control of speed.
811\end{remark}

820\subsection{Visibility and the seed package}\label{sec:corrugated-seed}
821For $|z|>R>0$ define the unit vector
822\begin{equation}\label{eq:visibility}
823 V_R(z)=\frac{Rz+\sqrt{|z|^2-R^2}\,Jz}{|z|^2}.
824\end{equation}
825Its numerator consists of perpendicular vectors with squared lengths
826summing to $|z|^4$.
827\begin{definition}\label{def:visible}
828Regular closed planar curves $(p,\delta)$ with a common parameter are
829\emph{visible at radius $R$} if
830\begin{equation}\label{eq:visible-pair}
831 |\delta|>R,\qquad p'\cdot V_R(\delta)>0,
832 \qquad \delta'\cdot V_R(\delta)>0.
833\end{equation}
834\end{definition}
835These conditions persist under common orientation-preserving
836reparametrization. On a compact parameter circle their strict margins
837also persist under small $C^1$ changes, or small positional changes with
838oriented tangent directions fixed. The connector construction will use
839visibility to replace a complicated gradient trace by a circle.
840
841Identify $\R^2$ with $\mathbb C$. For $N\ge2$, set
842$\epsilon=N^{-1}$, $T_N=2\pi/N$, and $\xi_N=e^{iT_N}$. Define
843\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrugated-beta}
844 \beta_N(t)=(1+\epsilon\sin Nt)
845 \exp\bigl(i[t+2\epsilon\sin Nt]\bigr).
846\end{equation}
847Choose $k_N$ as the unique root of
848\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrugated-root}
849 \int_0^{2\pi}e^{-k\cos x}(1+\epsilon\sin x)^2(1+2\cos x)\,dx=0,
850\end{equation}
851and put
852\[
853 Z_N=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{-k_N\cos x}\,dx,
854 \qquad m_N(t)=Z_N^{-1}e^{-k_N\cos Nt}.
855\]
856The partner curve is defined by integrating a positive multiple of the
857same tangent, with an initial point chosen for rotational covariance:
858\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrugated-delta}
859 I_N=\int_0^{T_N}m_N\beta_N'\,dt,
860 \qquad
861 \delta_N(t)=\frac{I_N}{\xi_N-1}+\int_0^t m_N(s)\beta_N'(s)\,ds.
862\end{equation}

864\begin{lemma}[Corrugated analytic seed]\label{lem:seed}
865For every integer $N\ge10^4$, $\beta_N$ and $\delta_N$ are real analytic
866positively oriented embedded curves enclosing the origin. If $Q_N$ is
867rotation through $2\pi/N$, then
868\begin{equation}\label{eq:seed-data}
869 \beta_N(t+T_N)=Q_N\beta_N(t),\qquad
870 \delta_N(t+T_N)=Q_N\delta_N(t),\qquad
871 \delta_N'=m_N\beta_N',\quad m_N>0,
872\end{equation}
873and
874\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-action}
875 \int_0^{2\pi}\det(\beta_N,\delta_N')\,dt=0.
876\end{equation}
877The pair $(\beta_N,\delta_N)$ is visible at $R_+=1/4$, and
878\[
879 (\widetilde\beta_N,\widetilde\delta_N)(t)
880 =\bigl(A_*\delta_N(-t),A_*\beta_N(-t)\bigr)
881\]
882is visible at $R_-=4/5$. The spherical lift
883\[
884 \zeta_N(t)=\frac{(\beta_N(t),1)}{\sqrt{1+|\beta_N(t)|^2}}
885\]
886is embedded in the northern hemisphere and its geodesic curvature takes
887both signs.
888\end{lemma}
889\begin{proof}
890Appendix~\ref{app:seed-estimates} verifies the root, covariance, action,
891embedding, visibility, and curvature assertions in that order.
892\end{proof}

903\subsection{From planar traces to a closed framed curve}\label{sec:model-curve}
904Fix one seed and suppress its subscript $N$. Let $Q$ denote rotation by
905$2\pi/N$, extended to $\R^3$ by fixing the third coordinate. The
906coordinate transitions used in this section are summarized here:
907\[
908\begin{array}{c|l}
909 t & \text{original seed parameter, of period }2\pi\\
910 r & \text{spherical arclength of }\zeta,\text{ of period }N\ell\\
911 a(r) & \text{positive spatial speed: }\Gamma_r=aP\\
912 s & \text{spatial arclength: }ds=a\,dr,\text{ of period }L\\
913 u & \text{distance along the ruling }E=\zeta_r\\
914 v & \text{transverse first integral labelling closed leaves}
915\end{array}
916\]
917Reparametrize $\zeta$ by $r$; covariance gives
918$\zeta(r+\ell)=Q\zeta(r)$. Its frame is
919\begin{equation}\label{eq:spherical-frame}
920 P=-\zeta\times\zeta_r,\qquad
921 \zeta_{rr}=-\zeta-\kappa P,\qquad P_r=\kappa\zeta_r.
922\end{equation}
923Both $\kappa$ and $|\beta|$ are $\ell$-periodic. With
924$w=\sqrt{1+|\beta|^2}$, the planar expression is
925\begin{equation}\label{eq:P-formula}
926 P=\frac{(-J\beta_r,-\det(\beta,\beta_r))}{w^2}.
927\end{equation}
928Define the spatial lift by
929\begin{equation}\label{eq:spatial-lift}
930 \Gamma_h=-J\delta,\qquad
931 (\Gamma_3)_t=-\det(\beta,\delta_t),
932\end{equation}
933where $h$ denotes horizontal components. The vertical integrand is
934cell-periodic. Its total integral is zero by \eqref{eq:zero-action}, so
935each of the $N$ equal cell integrals is zero. Thus the vertical primitive
936is periodic on every cell. Fix its additive constant. Since
937$\delta_r=m\beta_r$, \eqref{eq:P-formula} gives
938\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-speed}
939 \Gamma_r=a_0P,\qquad a_0=mw^2>0.
940\end{equation}
941Its horizontal projection is embedded, so $\Gamma$ is embedded.
942
943For any positive $\ell$-periodic speed $a$, closure reduces to the scalar
944moment
945\begin{equation}\label{eq:closing-moment}
946 M(a)=\int_0^\ell aP_3\,dr=0.
947\end{equation}
948Indeed, the horizontal cell increments are successive rotations and
949sum to zero; the vertical increments are all equal. Choose the horizontal
950initial point by
951\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariant-start}
952 (Q-I)\Gamma_h(0)=\int_0^\ell aP_h\,dr.
953\end{equation}
954Then $\Gamma(r+\ell)=Q\Gamma(r)$. The new planar trace
955$\delta_a=J\Gamma_h$ satisfies $(\delta_a)_r=(a/w^2)\beta_r$:
956changing speed preserves its oriented tangent direction. The initial
957speed recovers $\delta$ and satisfies $M(a_0)=0$.
958
959The return balance over one cell is
960\begin{equation}\label{eq:balance-moment}
961 \mathscr B_\ell(a)=\int_0^\ell\frac{\kappa_r}{\sqrt a}\,dr.
962\end{equation}
963We use $\mathscr B_\ell$ here to distinguish this scalar from the support
964tensor $B_H$.
965
966\begin{proposition}[One-slowdown holonomy correction]\label{prop:one-slowdown}
967If $b>0$ is $\ell$-periodic with $M(b)=0$, $\kappa$ is nonconstant,
968and $P_3\not\equiv0$, then for every $\eta>0$ a positive smooth
969$\ell$-periodic $a$ satisfies
970\[
971 \|a-b\|_{L^1(0,\ell)}<\eta,\qquad M(a)=0,
972 \qquad\mathscr B_\ell(a)=0.
973\]
974The change is supported in at most two arbitrarily short intervals.
975\end{proposition}
976\begin{proof}
977Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-balance} on the cell circle with
978$f=P_3$, $g=\kappa_r$, and $B_*=0$. The moment rank is one, and the
979periodic nonconstant $\kappa$ has derivative of both signs. One slowdown
980and one compensating control suffice.
981\end{proof}

983\begin{theorem}[Balanced embedded speed]\label{thm:spike}\label{lem:speed}
984For every $\eta_0>0$, the seed admits a positive smooth $\ell$-periodic
985speed $a$ with
986\begin{equation}\label{eq:balanced-speed}
987 \|a-a_0\|_{L^1(0,\ell)}<\eta_0,\qquad
988 M(a)=0,\qquad\mathscr B_\ell(a)=0.
989\end{equation}
990For small enough $\eta_0$, its spatial primitive and horizontal
991projection are embedded, and both visibility conditions persist.
992\end{theorem}
993\begin{proof}
994The seed has nonconstant $\kappa$. Also $P_3\not\equiv0$: otherwise
995$\det(\beta,\beta_r)=0$ would prevent $\beta$ from winding around zero.
996The proposition therefore gives the speed. For fixed $N$, the initial
997point in \eqref{eq:covariant-start} varies continuously with the cell
998integral, and integration of $aP$ gives uniform closeness of primitives.
999On short parameter arcs, a fixed linear functional is monotone along all
1000positive-speed primitives; on the remaining pairs, the original embedded
1001curve has positive separation. This proves embedding for the spatial
1002curve and its horizontal projection. Visibility, after division by positive
1003speeds, depends on positions and the fixed oriented tangent directions.
1004Its strict margins therefore survive the uniform positional change.
1005\end{proof}

1061\subsection{Changing return and finding a positive curve}
1062\begin{lemma}[Exit perturbation]\label{lem:exit}
1063Let a saddle support in the northern hemisphere have a positively oriented
1064embedded closed asymptotic curve enclosing the north pole. Suppose its
1065return germ is the identity and the mixed support entry, evaluated on
1066unit tangent and outward conormal, is positive. Inside any prescribed
1067collar, an arbitrarily small $C^2$ perturbation fixing the first jet on
1068the curve can make return attracting or repelling. The attracting choice
1069gives arbitrarily close positive closed curves on the exterior side;
1070the repelling choice gives them on the interior side.
1071\end{lemma}
1072\begin{proof}
1073\emph{Step 1: express the multiplier through the normal second jet.}
1074Parametrize the curve $q_0(r)$ by spherical arclength, of period
1075$\mathcal L$, and let $P=-q_0\times(q_0)_r$ be its outward conormal.
1076The Fermi map and metric are
1077\[
1078 q(r,t)=\cos t\,q_0(r)+\sin t\,P(r),\qquad
1079 h^2dr^2+dt^2,\qquad h=\cos t+\kappa(r)\sin t.
1080\]
1081Here $t>0$ is exterior and $(q_0)_{rr}=-q_0-\kappa P$.
1082Write $B_H=L\,dr^2+2M\,dr\,dt+N\,dt^2$, with
1083$(L,M,N)|_{t=0}=(0,a,c)$ and $a>0$. The coordinate expressions are
1084\[
1085 L=H_{rr}-\frac{h_r}{h}H_r+hh_tH_t+Hh^2,
1086 \quad M=H_{rt}-\frac{h_t}{h}H_r,\quad N=H_{tt}+H.
1087\]
1088On the seam they give
1089\begin{equation}\label{eq:seam-derivative}
1090 L_t=a_r+\kappa c.
1091\end{equation}
1092For clarity, $a_r=H_{rrt}-\kappa_rH_r-\kappa H_{rr}$ there;
1093subtracting this from $L_t$ leaves
1094$\kappa(H_{rr}+\kappa H_t+H)+\kappa(H_{tt}+H)=\kappa c$.
1095The asymptotic slope vanishing on the seam is
1096\[
1097 t_r=f(r,t)=-\frac{L}{M+\sqrt{M^2-LN}},\qquad
1098 f_t(r,0)=-\frac{a_r+\kappa c}{2a}.
1099\]
1100Integrating its variational equation gives
1101\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier}
1102 \mu=\exp\left(-\frac12\int_0^{\mathcal L}\frac{\kappa c}{a}\,dr\right),
1103\end{equation}
1104because the period of $a_r/a$ is zero.
1105
1106\emph{Step 2: change that jet while retaining the curve.}
1107Choose a cutoff $\chi(t)$ supported in the prescribed collar and equal
1108one near zero. Put
1109\[
1110 H_\epsilon=H+\frac\epsilon2a(r)\kappa(r)t^2\chi(t).
1111\]
1112The first jet is unchanged; $L=0$ and $M=a$ remain, while $c$ changes
1113to $c+\epsilon a\kappa$. Since the original multiplier is one,
1114\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturbed-multiplier}
1115 \mu_\epsilon=\exp\left(-\frac\epsilon2
1116 \int_0^{\mathcal L}\kappa^2\,dr\right).
1117\end{equation}
1118The integral is positive: otherwise the curve would be a great circle,
1119which cannot lie in an open hemisphere. Thus $\epsilon>0$ gives
1120attraction and $\epsilon<0$ repulsion. For fixed collar and cutoff, small
1121$\epsilon$ preserves the saddle determinant by $C^2$ continuity.
1122
1123\emph{Step 3: produce a positive closed graph on the chosen side.}
1124Fix such a nonzero $\epsilon$, set $b(r)=f_t(r,0)$ for the perturbed
1125support, and let $\bar b=(\log\mu_\epsilon)/\mathcal L$.
1126The zero-mean equation $v_r=(b-\bar b)v$ has a positive periodic solution.
1127For the graph $t=\delta v(r)$, uniform Taylor expansion gives
1128\[
1129 B_{H_\epsilon}(\partial_r+t_r\partial_t,
1130 \partial_r+t_r\partial_t)
1131 =-2ab\delta v+2a\delta v_r+O(\delta^2)
1132 =-2a\bar b\delta v+O(\delta^2).
1133\]
1134This is positive for small $\delta>0$ in the attracting case and small
1135$\delta<0$ in the repelling case. The graphs are embedded closed curves
1136in the collar and converge smoothly to the seam.
1137\end{proof}

1190\subsection{Relative smoothing at a positive seam}
1191\begin{lemma}[Relative saddle smoothing]\label{lem:smoothing}
1192Suppose two smooth planar potentials have matching values and gradients
1193on a compact embedded closed seam. If their Hessians are indefinite and
1194their common tangential quadratic form is positive, they admit a smooth
1195saddle interpolation equal to the old pieces outside any prescribed seam
1196neighborhood and arbitrarily close to the piecewise potential in $C^1$.
1197\end{lemma}
1198\begin{proof}
1199\emph{The allowed second jets form a convex interval.}
1200Use tubular coordinates $(s,t)$ with seam $t=0$ and denote the piecewise
1201function by $G_{\rm pw}$. Matching first jets makes it $C^1$ with locally
1202Lipschitz first derivatives. Differentiating the common gradient along
1203the seam shows that the tangential and mixed Hessian entries agree.
1204In a tangent-normal orthonormal frame the limiting Hessians are
1205\[
1206 H_\pm(s)=\begin{pmatrix}a(s)&b(s)\\b(s)&c_\pm(s)\end{pmatrix},
1207 \qquad a>0,\quad c_\pm<b^2/a.
1208\]
1209There is a uniform strict margin, and every convex combination remains
1210indefinite because only the normal entry varies.
1211
1212\emph{Mollify and retain the old germs.}
1213Extend the coordinate cylinder periodically in $s$ and convolve with a
1214symmetric nonnegative mollifier of radius $\delta$. Since the gradient
1215has no jump, the distributional Hessian has no seam measure. Close to
1216the seam, the mollified Hessian is a weighted average of nearby one-sided
1217Hessians, uniformly $o(1)$ from the displayed convex segments. Conversion
1218from coordinate Hessians to Euclidean Hessians adds only $o(1)$ errors,
1219since the gradients converge uniformly. Thus the determinant stays negative.
1220Away from the seam, ordinary $C^2$ convergence gives the same conclusion.
1221
1222Patch the mollified function to $G_{\rm pw}$ with a cutoff equal one for
1223$|t|\le h$ and zero for $|t|\ge2h$, where $h=\sqrt\delta$.
1224On the transition strips the mollifier stays on one smooth side.
1225Its value and gradient errors are $O(\delta^2)$ and $O(\delta)$.
1226The additional Hessian terms are therefore
1227$O(\delta^2h^{-2}+\delta h^{-1})=o(1)$. The saddle margin survives,
1228the $C^1$ error tends to zero, and the old pieces are unchanged for
1229$|t|\ge2h$. Taking $\delta$ small places all changes inside the prescribed
1230neighborhood.
1231\end{proof}
1232When using this lemma we cut the retained boundary inside an unchanged
1233incoming subcollar. Local smoothing gives no automatic global injectivity;
1234we verify it from the unchanged boundary images and the degree criterion.

1236\subsection{A connector with circular outgoing gradient}
1237The design is to draw a family of straight source segments from $p$,
1238choose their lengths so the terminal gradient is a circle, and then use
1239degree to rule out overlaps in both source and gradient.
1240\begin{lemma}[From a visible trace to a circular gradient]\label{lem:connector}
1241Let $p,\delta:\R/T\mathbb Z\to\R^2$ be positively oriented embedded
1242curves enclosing zero. Put $\gamma=-J\delta$ and assume
1243\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-data}
1244 p'\cdot\gamma'>0,\qquad g'=\gamma\cdot p'
1245\end{equation}
1246for a periodic $g$. If $(p,\delta)$ is visible at $R>0$, an exterior
1247source annulus carries a saddle potential with incoming value $g$ and
1248gradient $\gamma$, and outgoing gradient a positively parametrized
1249circle of radius $R$. Both boundaries are positive for the Hessian.
1250The source and gradient maps are diffeomorphisms of interiors onto their
1251respective planar annuli. If the terminal gradient is written
1252$Re_\theta$, its terminal source trace $P_\mathrm{out}(\theta)$ also
1253satisfies $P_\mathrm{out}\cdot e_\theta>0$ for a sufficiently small
1254connector parameter. If an incoming saddle germ supplies the data,
1255relative smoothing retains a smaller incoming collar and the outgoing germ.
1256\end{lemma}
1257\begin{proof}
1258\emph{Step 1: choose the segment directions and lengths.}
1259Set $\kappa_0=\sqrt{|\delta|^2-R^2}$ and choose an angular lift with
1260\begin{equation}\label{eq:visibility-angle}
1261 e_\varphi=V_R(\delta),\qquad
1262 \delta=Re_\varphi-\kappa_0Je_\varphi.
1263\end{equation}
1264Differentiation gives $\varphi'=\delta'\cdot e_\varphi/\kappa_0>0$.
1265The argument of $\delta$ differs from $\varphi$ by the periodic angle
1266$-\arctan(\kappa_0/R)$, so $\varphi(s+T)=\varphi(s)+2\pi$.
1267Choose a small constant $\eta>0$, put $\psi=\varphi-\eta$ and
1268$w=\delta-Re_\psi$, and define
1269\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-scalars}
1270 E_0=\delta\cdot e_\psi-R,\quad
1271 A_0=\det(w,p'),\quad B_0=\det(w,\delta'),\quad
1272 C_0=E_0R\psi',\quad \ell=A_0/C_0.
1273\end{equation}
1274All four scalars are positive for small $\eta$, uniformly in $s$:
1275$E_0=\kappa_0\sin\eta-R(1-\cos\eta)$,
1276$A_0\to\kappa_0p'\cdot e_\varphi>0$, and
1277$B_0\to\kappa_0\delta'\cdot e_\varphi>0$.
1278
1279\emph{Step 2: verify local potential and saddle determinant.}
1280On $0\le u\le\ell(s)$ put
1281\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-map}
1282 P(s,u)=p+uw,\qquad \Delta=A_0+u(B_0-C_0),\qquad
1283 \widehat G(s,u)=g+u\gamma\cdot w.
1284\end{equation}
1285At the endpoints $\Delta=A_0$ and $\Delta=\ell B_0$, hence $\Delta>0$.
1286Since $\det(w,w')=B_0-C_0$,
1287\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-jacobian}
1288 \det(P_s,P_u)=-\Delta<0.
1289\end{equation}
1290In a local inverse chart $G=\widehat G\circ P^{-1}$ has gradient
1291\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-gradient}
1292 Y=\gamma+\frac{uB_0}{\Delta}Jw,
1293 \qquad Y_u=\frac{A_0B_0}{\Delta^2}Jw.
1294\end{equation}
1295Indeed, $Y\cdot P_u=\widehat G_u$; also $Jw\cdot P_s=\Delta$ and
1296$\gamma'\cdot w=B_0$, so $Y\cdot P_s=\widehat G_s$.
1297The pulled-back Hessian has $uu$ entry zero and mixed entry
1298$A_0B_0/\Delta$. Dividing its determinant by the square of the source
1299Jacobian gives
1300\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-det}
1301 \det D^2G=-\frac{A_0^2B_0^2}{\Delta^4}<0.
1302\end{equation}
1303At $u=\ell$ the fraction in \eqref{eq:connector-gradient} is one, hence
1304\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-terminal}
1305 Y(s,\ell(s))=-RJ e_{\psi(s)}.
1306\end{equation}
1307
1308\emph{Step 3: prove global source injectivity.}
1309As $\eta\downarrow0$, convergence in each fixed smooth norm gives
1310\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector-limit}
1311 \eta\ell\longrightarrow z_0:=\frac{p'\cdot e_\varphi}{R\varphi'}>0,
1312 \qquad \eta P(s,\ell(s))\longrightarrow-z_0\kappa_0Je_\varphi.
1313\end{equation}
1314For $\theta=\psi-\pi/2$, the same limit gives
1315$\eta P(s,\ell(s))\cdot e_\theta\longrightarrow
1316z_0\kappa_0>0$. This is the radial-derivative condition needed after
1317Legendre duality. The limit is a radial graph with positive radius and
1318strictly increasing polar angle $\varphi-\pi/2$. Its radius and angular derivative have
1319positive minima. For small $\eta$ the terminal source curve is therefore
1320embedded, positively oriented, and star-shaped, and its unscaled radius
1321tends uniformly to infinity. It encloses $p$. The induced orientations
1322of the two boundary curves are opposite. Lemma~\ref{lem:degree}, together
1323with \eqref{eq:connector-jacobian}, gives global injectivity of $P$ and a
1324single well-defined potential on its source annulus.
1325
1326\emph{Step 4: check the seams and global gradient injectivity.}
1327Differentiating \eqref{eq:connector-limit} and pairing with $e_\psi$ yields
1328\[
1329 \eta\frac d{ds}P(s,\ell(s))\cdot e_\psi
1330 \longrightarrow z_0\kappa_0\varphi'>0.
1331\]
1332The outgoing gradient derivative is $R\psi'e_\psi$, so its tangential
1333Hessian is positive. The incoming one is positive by hypothesis.
1334The incoming gradient curve $\gamma=-J\delta$ encloses the disk of
1335radius $R$: it is embedded, surrounds zero, and stays outside that disk.
1336Thus the gradient boundary curves are disjoint and nested. Equation
1337\eqref{eq:connector-det} and the degree criterion give global gradient
1338injectivity. Finally the incoming first jet is exactly $(g,\gamma)$.
1339Relative saddle smoothing attaches any incoming germ while retaining
1340both an unchanged incoming subcollar and the outgoing germ. The boundary
1341images still provide the same degree argument on the joined annulus.
1342\end{proof}

1345\subsection{Quadratic filling of a circular source seam}
1346The next lemma replaces the separate straightening, logarithmic, and cap
1347constructions. Its proof uses two \emph{linear} inequalities within the
1348nonconvex saddle condition. For a function in polar coordinates,
1349\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-hessian}
1350 D^2 f\big|_{(e_r,e_\theta)}=
1351 \begin{pmatrix}
1352 f_{rr}& f_{r\theta}/r-f_\theta/r^2\\
1353 f_{r\theta}/r-f_\theta/r^2&(f_{\theta\theta}+rf_r)/r^2
1354 \end{pmatrix}.
1355\end{equation}
1356Thus $f_{rr}<0$ and $f_{\theta\theta}+rf_r>0$ imply
1357$\det D^2f<0$, regardless of the mixed derivative.
1358
1359\begin{lemma}[Quadratic filling at a circular seam]
1360\label{lem:quadratic-filling}
1361Let $f$ be a smooth saddle potential on an exterior collar of
1362$\{|y|=R\}$, with boundary traces
1363\[
1364 h(\theta)=f(Re_\theta),\qquad b(\theta)=f_r(Re_\theta).
1365\]
1366Assume
1367\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-fill-data}
1368 b(\theta)>0,\qquad h''(\theta)+Rb(\theta)>0.
1369\end{equation}
1370There is a smooth saddle extension into $0<|y|<R$, agreeing with $f$
1371outside any prescribed seam neighborhood, and equal near the puncture to
1372\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-germ}
1373 f(y)=c|y|-\frac M2|y|^2+d,\qquad c=MR,
1374\end{equation}
1375where $M>0$ can be arbitrarily large. If additionally the trace
1376$\nabla f(Re_\theta)$ is a positively oriented Jordan curve enclosing
1377zero, the extension can be chosen with globally injective gradient on
1378the attached annulus, and the radial inner-gradient circle can be made
1379to enclose that Jordan curve.
1380\end{lemma}
1381\begin{proof}
1382Choose a smooth function $\chi:[0,R]\to[0,1]$ equal to zero for
1383$r\le R/2$ and one for $r\ge3R/4$. On $0<r\le R$ put
1384\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-filling}
1385 f_M(r,\theta)=-\frac M2(r-R)^2
1386 +\chi(r)\bigl[h(\theta)+(r-R)b(\theta)\bigr].
1387\end{equation}
1388At $r=R$ this has exactly the prescribed value and first derivatives.
1389All the angular terms and their derivatives are bounded independently
1390of $M$, while
1391\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-radial-signs}
1392 (f_M)_{rr}=-M+O(1),\qquad
1393 (f_M)_{\theta\theta}+r(f_M)_r
1394 =Mr(R-r)+O(1).
1395\end{equation}
1396At $r=R$, the full second expression is $h''+Rb>0$ by
1397\eqref{eq:radial-fill-data}. By continuity and the nonnegative
1398leading term, it remains positive in a fixed narrow collar below
1399$R$ for large $M$. Off that collar the leading term
1400$Mr(R-r)$ is positive; on $r<R/2$ the angular error vanishes altogether.
1401Hence both strict inequalities preceding the lemma hold throughout the
1402punctured disk. Near $r=0$ formula \eqref{eq:quadratic-filling} is
1403exactly \eqref{eq:quadratic-germ} with $d=-MR^2/2$.
1404
1405The two potentials have a common first jet on $r=R$, indefinite
1406Hessians, and positive common tangential Hessian
1407$(h''+Rb)/R^2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing} gives a smooth
1408interpolation confined to an arbitrarily small seam collar, retaining
1409both the exterior original germ and the radial inner germ. First fix
1410$M$, then take that collar sufficiently small to keep the strict signs.
1411
1412For the injectivity assertion, write $P(\theta)=\nabla f(Re_\theta)$.
1413The positive Jordan curve $P$ persists on a nearby unchanged exterior
1414circle. Take a fixed small inner circle $r=r_0<R/2$. There
1415\[
1416 \nabla f_M(r_0e_\theta)=(MR-Mr_0)e_\theta.
1417\]
1418For large $M$ this circle surrounds $P$. Between the two boundaries
1419$\det D^2f<0$, the boundary maps are embedded and nested, and their
1420induced winding numbers are opposite. The annular degree criterion,
1421Lemma~\ref{lem:degree}, gives global gradient injectivity, also after
1422relative smoothing. The same argument applies to any shorter compact
1423annulus within the extension.
1424\end{proof}

1426\subsection{A radial neck adapter}
1427The quadratic germ can be ended at the puncture, or it can be joined
1428radially to a square-root germ. The latter has a particularly simple
1429inverse Legendre transform.
1430
1431\begin{lemma}[Square-root neck adapter]\label{lem:neck-adapter}
1432Suppose a radial saddle potential $f=f(r)$ has $f'>0$ and $f''<0$
1433near a circle $r=r_j>0$. Choose any $0<a<r_j$ and set
1434\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-adapter-constants}
1435 \ell=r_j-a,\quad B=f'(r_j)^2\ell,\quad
1436 C=f(r_j)-2f'(r_j)\ell.
1437\end{equation}
1438Then
1439\begin{equation}\label{eq:sqrt-neck}
1440 f_{\rm neck}(r)=C+2\sqrt{B(r-a)},\qquad a<r\le r_j,
1441\end{equation}
1442matches the value and radial derivative of $f$ at $r_j$, and satisfies
1443$f_{\rm neck}'>0$, $f_{\rm neck}''<0$. Relative radial smoothing gives
1444a smooth saddle continuation, unchanged near its original incoming
1445collar and exactly \eqref{eq:sqrt-neck} near $r=a$. The radial gradient
1446there has norm tending to infinity as $r\downarrow a$.
1447\end{lemma}
1448\begin{proof}
1449The derivative is $\sqrt{B/(r-a)}$, and the second derivative is
1450$-\sqrt B/(2(r-a)^{3/2})$. The constants in
1451\eqref{eq:neck-adapter-constants} give the matching first jet. In a
1452radial interpolation, the second derivative may be smoothed through
1453negative functions while the first derivative remains positive; this
1454is the one-dimensional special case of Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing}.
1455The radial Hessian eigenvalues $f''$ and $f'/r$ keep opposite signs.
1456On the square-root segment the radial gradient is strictly monotone
1457and becomes unbounded at $a$.
1458\end{proof}

1460\subsection{The two-sided extension theorem}
1461We now state explicitly what the global construction takes as input.
1462Neither the identity-return property nor a ruling is used in this
1463step. They were needed to create the protected band and its bendings,
1464not to extend a positive boundary jet.
1465
1466\begin{proposition}[Two-sided support completion]\label{prop:dual-radial-completion}
1467Suppose a compact saddle support annulus has injective source and
1468gradient maps, nested positively oriented source and gradient boundary
1469curves enclosing zero, and positive boundary traces as in
1470\eqref{eq:positive-traces}. Assume its outer boundary pair and its
1471reflected Legendre-dual inner pair obey the two visibility conditions
1472\eqref{eq:reflected-visible}. Then, after leaving an arbitrary
1473prescribed smaller protected annulus unchanged, it admits a saddle
1474support extension $\widetilde G$ on $\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}$ with
1475\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-two-ends}
1476 \begin{aligned}
1477 \widetilde G(p)&=R_N|p|-\frac\mu2|p|^2+d_0
1478 && (0<|p|\ll1),\\
1479 \widetilde G(p)&=A|p|-\frac B{|p|}+d_\infty
1480 && (|p|\gg1),
1481 \end{aligned}
1482\end{equation}
1483where $R_N>A>0$ and $\mu,B>0$. The gradient is a
1484diffeomorphism
1485\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-gradient-annulus}
1486 \nabla\widetilde G:\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}
1487 \longrightarrow \{y\in\mathbb R^2:A<|y|<R_N\}.
1488\end{equation}
1489Every smoothing takes place outside the protected region.
1490\end{proposition}
1491\begin{proof}
1492\emph{The exterior end.}
1493Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:connector} to $(p_+,J\gamma_+)$, retaining an
1494incoming collar. Its terminal gradient is $Re_\theta$, and the
1495terminal source trace $P(\theta)$ is a positive Jordan curve with
1496$P\cdot e_\theta>0$. Apply the differential Legendre transform
1497$f=G^*$ on the gradient image. Its source seam is the circle
1498$y=Re_\theta$ and
1499\[
1500 f_r(Re_\theta)=P\cdot e_\theta>0,\qquad
1501 h''+Rf_r=R P_\theta\cdot Je_\theta>0.
1502\]
1503The last inequality is precisely positivity of the connector's
1504outgoing seam. Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic-filling} therefore continues
1505$f$ radially inward, with injective gradient. Stop within the
1506quadratic radial region at $r=r_j$, and apply
1507Lemma~\ref{lem:neck-adapter}; choose its inner radius $a=A>0$.
1508The gradient of this dual continuation tends radially to infinity at
1509$r=A$. Inverse Legendre transformation consequently extends the
1510original potential toward $|p|=\infty$. Indeed, on the terminal
1511square-root germ $|p|=f_{\rm neck}'(r)$, so
1512$r=A+B/|p|^2$ and
1513\[
1514 p\cdot y-f_{\rm neck}(y)=A|p|-B/|p|-C.
1515\]
1516This is the second germ in \eqref{eq:radial-two-ends}.
1517Every stage agrees with the preceding one on an unchanged open collar.
1518
1519\emph{The interior end.}
1520Use reflected differential duality: put $K(x)=G^*(Sx)$. In terms
1521of the original inner traces $(p_-,\gamma_-)$, the new source and
1522gradient traces, with reversed parameter, are respectively
1523\[
1524 p^-(s)=S\gamma_-(-s),\qquad \gamma^-(s)=Sp_-(-s).
1525\]
1526The action period still vanishes, since
1527$\oint p_-\cdot d\gamma_-=-\oint\gamma_-\cdot dp_-=0$.
1528Reflection and parameter reversal preserve the positive orientation
1529of the two traces. The source-gradient nesting reverses, so its
1530exterior extension corresponds to filling the bounded source side
1531of the original inner exit. Its trace meets the same connector
1532hypotheses by \eqref{eq:reflected-visible}. Run the connector, then
1533take $f=K^*$ and use Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic-filling} down to the
1534puncture. On the unchanged initial collar
1535\[
1536 K^*(y)=G(Sy),
1537\]
1538so the inverse reflection recovers the original $G$. The radial
1539quadratic germ of $K^*$ is invariant under $S$, giving the first
1540germ in \eqref{eq:radial-two-ends}. Its leading radius
1541$R_N=\mu R$ can be made arbitrarily large; we choose it larger than
1542$A$ and than the bounded intermediate gradient traces.
1543
1544\emph{Global geometry and periods.}
1545The source annuli produced at the successive stages are nested on
1546the required sides, and the old source and gradient maps were
1547injective. On each concatenated dual annulus, the outer source trace
1548and the newly added inner source circle are disjoint and nested; their
1549gradient images are likewise nested by the connector and the choice
1550of a large quadratic coefficient. Lemma~\ref{lem:degree} therefore
1551makes the continued dual gradients injective, as required for each
1552inverse Legendre transformation. The only changes to potentials at
1553interfaces are the relative smoothings above. On the inner and outer radial collars,
1554for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ and large $L$,
1555\[
1556 \nabla\widetilde G(\varepsilon e_\theta)
1557 =(R_N-\mu\varepsilon)e_\theta,
1558 \qquad
1559 \nabla\widetilde G(Le_\theta)
1560 =(A+B/L^2)e_\theta.
1561\]
1562The boundary circles are disjoint and nested, and the Hessian
1563Jacobian is negative on the intervening source annulus.
1564Lemma~\ref{lem:degree} proves that its gradient is a
1565diffeomorphism onto the intervening planar annulus. Exhausting the
1566punctured plane establishes \eqref{eq:full-gradient-annulus}.
1567All Legendre transformations are genuine differentials of globally
1568injective gradients on the relevant compact annuli; they therefore
1569introduce no missing periods. The inner and outer potentials recover
1570the original value and gradient on unchanged overlaps, so they
1571assemble into one globally defined support.
1572\end{proof}

1576\section{From the radial support to a tight torus}\label{sec:model-saddle}
1577Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-radial-completion} gives two explicit end
1578germs without passing through a logarithmic support. One end resolves
1579into a circular boundary, the other into a reflectable parabolic neck.
1580We then close the resulting negative-curvature annulus by a convex
1581annulus. This stage uses only the support geometry and does not alter

1584\subsection{Circular compactification and reflection}
1585Let $\widetilde G$ be the completed potential in
1586\eqref{eq:radial-two-ends}, and put $r=|p|$.
1587Using \eqref{eq:planar}, its image near the puncture is
1588\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-polar-immersion}
1589 X_{\widetilde G}(r,\theta)=
1590 ((R_N-\mu r)e_\theta,\ d_0+\tfrac\mu2r^2).
1591\end{equation}
1592Near infinity it is
1593\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-neck-immersion}
1594 X_{\widetilde G}(r,\theta)=
1595 ((A+B/r^2)e_\theta,\ d_\infty-2B/r).
1596\end{equation}
1597Negate the immersion, translate it vertically by $d_\infty$, and
1598shift the angular parameter by $\pi$. At the puncture, write $t=-r$;
1599then the northern boundary collar is
1600\begin{equation}\label{eq:ring-N}
1601 F_N(\theta,t)=(R_N+\mu t)e_\theta
1602 +\bigl(h-\tfrac\mu2t^2\bigr)e_3,
1603 \qquad -\delta<t\le0,
1604\end{equation}
1605where $h=d_\infty-d_0$. At the other end put $\eta=1/r$.
1606The neck is
1607\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-parabolic-neck}
1608 X(\theta,\eta)=(A+B\eta^2)e_\theta+2B\eta e_3,
1609 \qquad \eta\ge0,
1610\end{equation}
1611so its radius and height satisfy
1612\begin{equation}\label{eq:parabolic-profile}
1613 \rho_{\rm spatial}=A+\frac{z^2}{4B}.
1614\end{equation}
1615It is therefore smoothly reflected across $z=0$.
1616
1617The puncture $p=0$ is \emph{not} filled by one surface point: it
1618resolves into a whole boundary circle. In \eqref{eq:ring-N}, the
1619angular and transverse derivatives at $t=0$ are
1620$R_NJe_\theta$ and $\mu e_\theta$, so the immersion is regular.
1621The neck \eqref{eq:new-parabolic-neck} is also regular at $\eta=0$,
1622where the corresponding derivatives are $AJe_\theta$ and $2Be_3$.
1623
1624\begin{proposition}[Completed saddle annulus]\label{prop:saddle}
1625The completed support compactifies, after reflection at its neck,
1626to a smooth embedded annulus $N$ with $K<0$ in its interior. Its
1627boundary circles have common radius $R_N$, at heights $\pm h$ with
1628$h>0$. Near them its collars are \eqref{eq:ring-N} and
1629\begin{equation}\label{eq:ring-S}
1630 F_S(\theta,t)=(R_N+\mu t)e_\theta
1631 +\bigl(-h+\tfrac\mu2t^2\bigr)e_3,
1632 \qquad -\delta<t\le0.
1633\end{equation}
1634On either collar,
1635\begin{equation}\label{eq:ring-curvature}
1636 K=\frac{t}{\mu(R_N+\mu t)(1+t^2)^2}.
1637\end{equation}
1638All bending fields supported in the protected region extend by zero
1639over the annulus, and vanish on full boundary collars.
1640\end{proposition}
1641\begin{proof}
1642\emph{Embeddedness of the upper half.}
1643By \eqref{eq:full-gradient-annulus} its horizontal projection is
1644globally injective on the punctured source. The two displayed end
1645germs show that projection extends to nested disjoint boundary
1646circles of radii $A<R_N$, with regular full surface collars.
1647Consequently the compactified upper half is embedded.
1648
1649\emph{Height separation.}
1650The interior Gauss normal is represented by
1651$q(p)=(p,1)/\sqrt{1+|p|^2}$, up to orientation, and is never vertical
1652for $0<|p|<\infty$. Hence the vertical height has no interior
1653critical point. Formula \eqref{eq:new-parabolic-neck} gives strictly
1654positive height close to the lower neck circle, while
1655\eqref{eq:ring-N} gives height strictly below $h$ near the upper
1656circle. The maximum and minimum on the compact annulus therefore
1657occur at these boundary components. It follows that
1658\[
1659 h>0,\qquad 0<X_3<h\quad\text{in the upper interior}.
1660\]
1661The reflection at the even neck \eqref{eq:parabolic-profile} is
1662smooth; the two halves have disjoint interiors, so their union is
1663embedded and has negative curvature throughout the interior.
1664
1665\emph{Boundary curvature and retained fields.}
1666For \eqref{eq:ring-N}, set $r(t)=R_N+\mu t$ and
1667$z(t)=h-\mu t^2/2$. A unit normal is
1668$(t e_\theta+e_3)/\sqrt{1+t^2}$; the metric coefficients are
1669$r(t)^2$ and $\mu^2(1+t^2)$, and the second fundamental
1670coefficients are $-r(t)t/\sqrt{1+t^2}$ and
1671$-\mu/\sqrt{1+t^2}$. This proves \eqref{eq:ring-curvature}.
1672Reflection gives the same intrinsic formula at the southern end.
1673Every change was outside the protected region, where all retained
1674bendings vanish on a collar; extension by zero is therefore smooth
1675and preserves their linearized metric equations.
1676\end{proof}

1678\subsection{Convex closure}\label{sec:model-convex}
1679The parabolic collars now replace the circular arcs of a standard
1680torus of revolution. Their common value and slope make the positive
1681curvature closure a one-dimensional concavity problem.
1682
1683\begin{lemma}[Convex closure]\label{lem:convex}
1684For any $R_N,\mu,h>0$, there exists a convex body whose boundary
1685consists of the disks
1686\[
1687 D_\pm=\{(v,\pm h):|v|\le R_N\}
1688\]
1689and a smooth annulus $U_{\rm sp}$ with strictly positive curvature
1690in its interior. The annulus agrees with \eqref{eq:ring-N} and
1691\eqref{eq:ring-S} for $0\le t<\delta$, has outward Gauss map
1692\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-gauss}
1693 \nu:U_{\rm sp}^{\circ}\longrightarrow
1694 \mathbb S^2\setminus\{\pm e_3\}
1695\end{equation}
1696a diffeomorphism, and satisfies
1697\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-exterior}
1698 U_{\rm sp}^{\circ}\subset
1699 \{\rho_{\rm spatial}>R_N,\ |z|<h\},
1700 \qquad U_{\rm sp}\ne -U_{\rm sp}.
1701\end{equation}
1702\end{lemma}
1703\begin{proof}
1704Choose a strictly concave, even function $r_0$ on $[-h,h]$ with
1705$r_0(\pm h)=R_N$, $r_0>R_N$ in the interior, and near the endpoints
1706\begin{equation}\label{eq:meridian-radius}
1707 r_0(z)=R_N+\sqrt{2\mu(h-z)}\quad(z\text{ near }h),
1708 \qquad
1709 r_0(z)=R_N+\sqrt{2\mu(h+z)}\quad(z\text{ near }-h).
1710\end{equation}
1711For completeness, on $[0,h]$ choose a smooth strictly increasing
1712$v=-r_0'$ with $v(z)=\epsilon z$ near zero and
1713$v(z)=\sqrt{\mu/[2(h-z)]}$ near $h$; interpolate monotonically
1714between those germs and set
1715$r_0(z)=R_N+\int_z^h v(s)\,ds$. The expression is smooth after
1716even reflection at zero. It is strictly concave and agrees with
1717\eqref{eq:meridian-radius} at the upper endpoint.
1718
1719Choose a nonzero smooth $b\ge0$ supported in $(0,h)$ and set
1720$r=r_0+\lambda b$. For sufficiently small $\lambda>0$ it remains
1721strictly concave, has unchanged end germs, and is no longer even.
1722The body $\mathcal K=\{(v,z):|z|\le h,\ |v|\le r(z)\}$ is convex,
1723since $r$ is concave. Its lateral annulus $(r(z)e_\theta,z)$ has
1724\[
1725 \nu=\frac{(e_\theta,-r'(z))}{\sqrt{1+r'(z)^2}},\qquad
1726 K=-\frac{r''(z)}{r(z)(1+r'(z)^2)^2}>0.
1727\]
1728As $z$ increases, $r'$ decreases from $+\infty$ to $-\infty$,
1729which gives the stated Gauss diffeomorphism. The upper equality in
1730\eqref{eq:meridian-radius}, together with
1731$z=h-\mu t^2/2$, gives $r=R_N+\mu t$ for $t\ge0$;
1732the southern equality works identically. Thus the joins preserve
1733full smooth germs. Since $r(z)>R_N$ for $|z|<h$, the lateral annulus
1734lies outside the prescribed cylinder, and non-evenness excludes
1735central symmetry.
1736\end{proof}
1737
1738The global gradient image in \eqref{eq:full-gradient-annulus} gives
1739$\rho_{\rm spatial}<R_N$ on $N^\circ$, and the height separation
1740in Proposition~\ref{prop:saddle} gives $|z|<h$ there. Hence
1741\[
1742 N\subset\mathcal C:=\{\rho_{\rm spatial}\le R_N,\ |z|\le h\},
1743\]
1744with strict inclusion for interior points. By
1745\eqref{eq:convex-exterior}, $U_{\rm sp}$ meets $N$ only along their
1746two boundary circles. Their parabolic germs coincide for negative
1747and positive $t$, producing a smooth embedded torus.
1748The curvature signs are negative on $N^\circ$, positive on
1749$U_{\rm sp}^\circ$, and zero exactly on the two joining circles.
1750By the Gauss area formula and \eqref{eq:convex-gauss},
1751\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-integral}
1752 \int_{U_{\rm sp}^\circ}K\,dA=4\pi.
1753\end{equation}
1754Gauss--Bonnet gives total absolute curvature $8\pi$, hence
1755tightness. This uses the standard tightness characterization in
1756terms of total absolute curvature \cite{BK}.

1759\subsection{An asymmetric fixed region}
1760In this paper a \emph{marked} surface means that the unchanged
1761positive-curvature region has trivial ambient Euclidean stabilizer.
1762This geometric condition rules out congruence of images; it does not
1763merely restrict allowed parametrizations. For a set $A\subset\R^3$,
1764$\Stab_{\Isom(\R^3)}A$ is the group of Euclidean isometries carrying
1765$A$ onto itself.
1766
1767\begin{lemma}[Small affine marking]\label{lem:ellipses-new}
1768For every sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, the map
1769\[
1770 A_\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}
1771 1&0&\epsilon\\0&1+\epsilon&2\epsilon\\0&0&1
1772 \end{pmatrix}
1773\]
1774tends to $\Id$ and satisfies
1775$\Stab_{\Isom(\R^3)}A_\epsilon(U_{\rm sp}^\circ)=\{\Id\}$.
1776\end{lemma}
1777\begin{proof}
1778An isometry preserving the open annulus preserves its closure and permutes
1779its two boundary ellipses. Their centers are
1780$\pm h(\epsilon,2\epsilon,1)$; their planes are horizontal; their unequal
1781semiaxes lie along $e_1,e_2$ and have lengths $R_N,(1+\epsilon)R_N$.
1782The midpoint of the centers is fixed, so the translation is zero.
1783The planes and principal axes force the orthogonal part to be diagonal
1784with entries $\pm1$. If both centers are fixed, all entries are $+1$,
1785because each coordinate of $(\epsilon,2\epsilon,1)$ is nonzero.
1786If they are exchanged, all are $-1$. This would give central inversion
1787of the annulus and hence $U_{\rm sp}=-U_{\rm sp}$, contrary to
1788Lemma~\ref{lem:convex}. Only the identity remains.
1789\end{proof}

1860\section{Exact metric branching}\label{sec:reconstruction}
1861The geometry is now in place. We first prove the finite sign mechanism;
1862the countable construction will use the same argument plus smooth
1863convergence and a compactness argument for the quotient topology.

1865\subsection{Finite independent signs}
1866The metric reconstruction map has the exact quadratic expansion
1867\begin{equation}\label{eq:metricquad}
1868 \Gmap(X+y)=\Gmap(X)+S_Xy+\langle dy,dy\rangle.
1869\end{equation}
1870\begin{lemma}[Disjoint quadratic branching]\label{thm:branch}
1871If $y_1,\ldots,y_m\in\ker S_X$ have pairwise disjoint supports, then
1872\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstractsign}
1873 g_{X+\sum_{j=1}^m\varepsilon_j a_jy_j}
1874 =g_X+\sum_{j=1}^m a_j^2\langle dy_j,dy_j\rangle,
1875 \qquad\varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}.
1876\end{equation}
1877The same identity holds for an infinite series converging in $C^\infty$.
1878\end{lemma}
1879\begin{proof}
1880Every linear term vanishes by the strain equation. A derivative is zero
1881off its field's support, so disjoint supports kill every mixed quadratic
1882term pointwise. This proves the finite formula. Smooth convergence allows
1883passage to the limit in the metric and its derivatives.
1884\end{proof}
1885
1886Two separate issues remain: tightness of the perturbed embeddings, and
1887noncongruence of their images. Both are controlled by the unchanged region.
1888\begin{proposition}[Relative preservation of absolute curvature]\label{prop:budget}
1889Let $X$ be a closed embedded surface and $V\Subset\{K_X<0\}$.
1890Every sufficiently small $C^2$ perturbation supported in $V$ remains an
1891embedding and has the same total absolute curvature. If $X$ is tight,
1892so is the perturbation.
1893\end{proposition}
1894\begin{proof}
1895Compactness and smallness preserve negative curvature on $V$ and preserve
1896embedding. Off $V$ the map is unchanged, so the positive-curvature region
1897$P$ and its curvature integral are unchanged. For either surface,
1898Gauss--Bonnet gives
1899\[
1900 \int_M|K|\,dA=2\int_P K\,dA-2\pi\chi(M).
1901\]
1902Thus the absolute curvature is unchanged, and equality in
1903\eqref{eq:tight-bound} preserves tightness.
1904\end{proof}

1906\begin{lemma}[An intrinsic isometry fixed on an open set]\label{lem:fixed-open}
1907An isometry of a connected smooth Riemannian manifold which fixes a
1908nonempty open set pointwise is the identity. The same holds for a connected
1909manifold with boundary when the fixed set meets its interior.
1910\end{lemma}
1911\begin{proof}
1912In the interior, the set of points fixed with identity differential is
1913nonempty and closed. It is open by the exponential-map relation
1914$f(\exp_pv)=\exp_{f(p)}(df_pv)$. Connectedness propagates the identity.
1915For a manifold with boundary, apply this to its connected interior and
1916then use continuity at the boundary.
1917\end{proof}

1919\begin{proposition}[Finite sign families]\label{prop:finite-sign}
1920Let $X:M\to\R^3$ be a tight embedding of a closed connected surface,
1921with $P=\{K_X>0\}$ and $\Stab_{\Isom(\R^3)}X(P)=\{\Id\}$.
1922Let $y_1,\ldots,y_m$ be nonzero bendings with disjoint supports in one
1923compact $V\Subset\{K_X<0\}$. For all sufficiently small positive
1924$a_j$, the $2^m$ maps in \eqref{eq:abstractsign} are tight embeddings
1925with a common metric, agree off $V$, and have pairwise noncongruent images.
1926\end{proposition}
1927\begin{proof}
1928The quadratic lemma gives the common metric and the curvature proposition
1929gives tight embeddings. Suppose an ambient isometry carries one image
1930onto another. Gaussian curvature is preserved, so it must carry the
1931unchanged positive region $X(P)$ onto itself. The stabilizer hypothesis
1932makes that ambient isometry the identity.
1933
1934The images therefore coincide. If their parametrizations are $X_\varepsilon$
1935and $X_\delta$, then $\varphi=X_\delta^{-1}\circ X_\varepsilon$ is an
1936isometry of the common intrinsic metric. It fixes $P$ pointwise because
1937both maps equal $X$ there. The fixed-open-set lemma gives $\varphi=\Id$.
1938Thus the maps coincide pointwise. On a point where $y_j\ne0$, all other
1939fields vanish, so $\varepsilon_j=\delta_j$. This holds for every $j$.
1940\end{proof}
1941For $m=1$, this is already the exact nonuniqueness assertion. Increasing
1942$m$ repeats the same localized mechanism independently.

1944\subsection{Countably many signs and the quotient topology}
1945\begin{lemma}[Hausdorff Euclidean orbit space]\label{lem:orbit-space}
1946For a closed Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$,
1947$\Emb_g(M,\R^3)/\Isom(\R^3)$ is metrizable and hence Hausdorff.
1948\end{lemma}
1949\begin{proof}
1950Subtract the mean of each embedding with respect to the Riemannian
1951volume $d\mu_g$. Centering removes
1952translations, so the quotient is the quotient of centered embeddings by
1953$O(3)$. On that space use the invariant metric
1954\[
1955 d(f,h)=\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k-1}\min\{1,\|f-h\|_{C^k(g)}\}.
1956\]
1957Since $O(3)$ is compact, the orbit distance
1958$\bar d([f],[h])=\min_{Q\in O(3)}d(f,Qh)$ is a metric. Invariant metric

2116$[H,\infty)$ equal to $q_0$ near $H$, with $q,q',q''>0$, and with

2443to the full period, completing \eqref{eq:corrugated-estimates}.

2445\subsection{The two visibility margins}
2446At $\delta_0=(i/2)e^{it}$,
2447\[
2448 V_{1/4}(\delta_0)=e^{it}(-\sqrt3/2+i/2).
2449\]
2450Thus
2451\begin{equation}\label{eq:outer-limit-visibility}
2452 U_0\cdot V_{1/4}(\delta_0)
2453 =\frac{1+(2-\sqrt3)c}{2L(c)}
2454 \ge\frac{\sqrt3-1}{2\sqrt{10}}>\frac1{10}.
2455\end{equation}
2456For the reflected reversed pair, the identity $A_*J=-JA_*$ reduces
2457visibility to positivity of
2458\[
2459 U_N\cdot\frac{-R_-\beta_N+
2460 \sqrt{|\beta_N|^2-R_-^2}\,J\beta_N}{|\beta_N|^2}.
2461\]
2462At $\beta_0=e^{it}$ and $R_-=4/5$ the limiting expression is
2463\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner-limit-visibility}
2464 \frac{-4c+3(1+2c)}{5L(c)}
2465 =\frac{3+2c}{5L(c)}\ge\frac1{5\sqrt{10}}>\frac1{16}.
2466\end{equation}
2467The differential norm of either direction map
2468$z\mapsto(\pm Rz+\sqrt{|z|^2-R^2}Jz)/|z|^2$ is
2469$1/\sqrt{|z|^2-R^2}$. For $N\ge10^4$, the comparison segments in
2470\eqref{eq:corrugated-estimates} have radii at least $0.494$ for the
2471outer test and $0.9997$ for the inner test. A Lipschitz bound of $3$
2472therefore suffices. The respective errors are at most
2473$50/N+3(60/N)=230/N$ and $50/N+3(3/N)=59/N$.
2474Both are smaller than their displayed margins, and both radius
2475inequalities hold. Since the velocities in each pair are positive
2476multiples, both required tangent inequalities follow. The positive-partner
2477lemma proves embedding and enclosure for $\delta_N$.

2479\subsection{The spherical curvature is nonconstant}
2480The gnomonic lift is embedded and stays in the northern hemisphere.
2481With the sign convention \eqref{eq:spherical-frame}, its geodesic
2482curvature has the sign of $\det(\beta_N',\beta_N'')$. Writing
2483$r=1+\epsilon\sin Nt$, $c=\cos Nt$, and $s=\sin Nt$, differentiation gives
2484\[
2485 \det(\beta_N',\beta_N'')
2486 =Nr s+2c^2(1+2c)+r^2(1+2c)^3.
2487\]
2488The values at $Nt=0$ and $Nt=\pi$ are $33$ and $-3$.
2489The curvature therefore takes both signs. This finishes every assertion
2490of Lemma~\ref{lem:seed}.
